# F4: Proof-of-income (NOT credit scoring) for informal South African vendors: how SA law treats income verification vs credit information (NCA/NCR registration triggers), POPIA obligations for processing income/trade evidence (including whether it is special personal information), what counts as valid proof of income for informal/cash traders (bank statements, till slips, stokvel records, affidavits, buyer attestations), and the fastest credible WhatsApp-based POI evidence product that can be demoed in 72 hours.

## Findings

### [1] Under NCA s40(1) a person must register as a credit provider if total principal debt owed to them under outstanding credit agreements (other than incidental credit agreements) exceeds the s42 threshold, and that threshold is currently Nil (R0) per GN 513, G. 39981 of 11 May 2016 — so any arm's-length consumer lending is in-scope for NCR credit-provider registration, while pure income-evidence services that do not grant credit are not.
- quote: "A person must apply to be registered as a credit provider if the total principal debt owed to that credit provider under all outstanding credit agreements, other than incidental credit agreements, exceeds the threshold prescribed in terms of section 42 (1). ... *Nil (R0) - GN 513, G. 39981, 11 May 2016."
- url: https://www.saflii.org/za/legis/consol_act/nca34o2005171/
- source_type: primary
- published: updated to 30 June 2021 (SAFLII last checked 27 March 2026)
- confidence: high

### [2] NCA s43(1) requires NCR registration as a credit bureau only where a person, for payment and other than as a credit provider/employee, receives or investigates reports of credit applications/agreements/payment history/consumer credit information, compiles and maintains that data, and issues reports — a POI product that collects vendor-submitted evidence and returns a one-off evidence pack to the vendor/partner without building a bureau-style multi-lender file sits outside this trigger if it is not the main business of retaining credit information.
- quote: "A person must apply to be registered as a credit bureau if that person engages for payment, other than as a credit provider or an employee of a credit provider, in the business of— (a) receiving reports of, or investigating— (i) credit applications; (ii) credit agreements; (iii) payment history or patterns; or (iv) consumer credit information as defined in section 70 (1), ... (b) compiling and maintaining data from reports ... and (c) issuing reports concerning consumers ..."
- url: https://www.saflii.org/za/legis/consol_act/nca34o2005171/
- source_type: primary
- published: updated to 30 June 2021
- confidence: high

### [3] NCA s70(1)(b) expressly classifies a person's financial history — including "past and current income" — as "consumer credit information" when held/reported by a registered credit bureau, so income facts become NCA-regulated credit information at the point they are compiled and reported bureau-style, not merely because they are collected for underwriting.
- quote: "\"consumer credit information\" means information concerning— ... (b) a person's financial history, including the person's past and current income, assets and debts, and other matters within the scope of that person's financial means, prospects and obligations, as defined in section 78 (3), and related matters"
- url: https://www.saflii.org/za/legis/consol_act/nca34o2005171/
- source_type: primary
- published: updated to 30 June 2021
- confidence: high

### [4] NCA s70(2)(h) forbids a credit bureau from drawing a negative inference or issuing a negative creditworthiness assessment merely because it holds no consumer credit information on a person — a statutory thin-file protection that supports reframing away from credit scores toward affirmative income evidence for informal vendors.
- quote: "not draw a negative inference about, or issue a negative assessment of, a person's creditworthiness merely on the basis that the credit bureau has no consumer credit information concerning that person"
- url: https://www.saflii.org/za/legis/consol_act/nca34o2005171/
- source_type: primary
- published: updated to 30 June 2021
- confidence: high

### [5] NCA s157C makes it an offence to hold oneself out as a credit bureau without s43 registration, with a limited exception where the act was a once-off transaction or incidental to the main business and that main business is not the retention of credit information — the key offence risk if KasiCred markets "Loan Ready" scores or a bureau-like rating.
- quote: "Any person who intentionally gives him or herself out to be— ... (c) a credit bureau, without having been registered under section 43 ... is guilty of an offence. ... (c) the act in question was a once-off transaction or incidental to the main business of the person ... Provided that the main business of that person may not be the provision of credit, debt counselling, payment distribution or alternative dispute resolution as contemplated in this Act, nor may it be the retention of credit information as contemplated in this Act."
- url: https://www.saflii.org/za/legis/consol_act/nca34o2005171/
- source_type: primary
- published: National Credit Amendment Act 7 of 2019 insertions (uncommenced portions marked; s157C text is in consolidated Act)
- confidence: high

### [6] NCA s54 lets the National Credit Regulator issue a stop notice to any person engaging in an activity that requires registration without being registered, and failure to comply with that notice is an offence — the enforcement lever if a demo looks like unlicensed credit scoring or unregistered credit provision.
- quote: "the National Credit Regulator may issue a notice ... requiring that person or association to stop engaging in, offering to engage in or holding themselves out as authorised to engage in, that activity. ... Failure to comply with a notice issued in terms of this section is an offence."
- url: https://www.saflii.org/za/legis/consol_act/nca34o2005171/
- source_type: primary
- published: updated to 30 June 2021
- confidence: high

### [7] NCA s78(3)(a) defines "financial means, prospects and obligations" to include income "regardless of the source, frequency or regularity of that income" (excluding trust-held income), and s78(3)(c) adds reasonably estimated future revenue flow where the consumer has a commercial purpose — so informal/cash trade income is legally cognisable for affordability without needing a payslip.
- quote: "\"financial means, prospects and obligations\", with respect to a consumer or prospective consumer, includes— (a) income, or any right to receive income, regardless of the source, frequency or regularity of that income, other than income that the consumer or prospective consumer receives, has a right to receive, or holds in trust for another person; ... (c) if the consumer has or had a commercial purpose for applying for or entering into a particular credit agreement, the reasonably estimated future revenue flow from that business purpose."
- url: https://www.saflii.org/za/legis/consol_act/nca34o2005171/
- source_type: primary
- published: s78 commencement 1 June 2007
- confidence: high

### [8] NCA s81(2) places the duty to assess a consumer's existing financial means, prospects and obligations (and debt repayment history) on the credit provider before entering a credit agreement, and s82 lets the credit provider choose evaluative mechanisms subject to affordability assessment regulations — income verification is an underwriting input for the lender, not a bureau score the vendor product must produce.
- quote: "A credit provider must not enter into a credit agreement without first taking reasonable steps to assess— (a) the proposed consumer's— ... (iii) existing financial means, prospects and obligations; ... A credit provider may determine for itself the evaluative mechanisms or models and procedures to be used in meeting its assessment obligations under section 81 ... The Minister must, on recommendation of the National Credit Regulator, make affordability assessment regulations."
- url: https://www.saflii.org/za/legis/consol_act/nca34o2005171/
- source_type: primary
- published: ss81–82 commencement 1 June 2007; s82 substituted by Act 19 of 2014 (13 March 2015)
- confidence: high

### [9] NCA s1 defines "stokvel" as a formal or informal rotating financial scheme that raises a continuous pool of member subscriptions and grants credit to members, and expressly excludes a stokvel from the definition of "juristic person" — so stokvel membership/subscription records are a recognised informal-finance artefact and stokvels are not treated as ordinary juristic credit consumers under the NCA.
- quote: "\"juristic person\" includes a partnership, association or other body of persons, corporate or unincorporated, or a trust if— ... but does not include a stokvel; ... \"stokvel\" means a formal or informal rotating financial scheme with entertainment, social or economic functions, which— (a) consists of two or more persons in a voluntary association ... (b) establishes a continuous pool of capital by raising funds by means of the subscriptions of the members; (c) grants credit to and on behalf of members ..."
- url: https://www.saflii.org/za/legis/consol_act/nca34o2005171/
- source_type: primary
- published: updated to 30 June 2021
- confidence: high

### [10] POPIA s26 "special personal information" is limited to religious/philosophical beliefs, race or ethnic origin, trade union membership, political persuasion, health or sex life, biometric information, and criminal behaviour/alleged offences — income, trade evidence, bank statements and till slips are ordinary personal information (s1 financial history), not special personal information, so s26 prohibition does not apply to POI collection.
- quote: "A responsible party may, subject to section 27, not process personal information concerning— (a) the religious or philosophical beliefs, race or ethnic origin, trade union membership, political persuasion, health or sex life or biometric information of a data subject; or (b) the criminal behaviour of a data subject to the extent that such information relates to— (i) the alleged commission by a data subject of any offence ..."
- url: https://popia.co.za/section-26-prohibition-on-processing-of-special-personal-information/
- source_type: primary
- published: POPIA Act 4 of 2013 (assented 19 Nov 2013; s26 in force 1 July 2020)
- confidence: high

### [11] POPIA s1 classifies information relating to a person's "financial" history as personal information and defines special personal information by reference to s26 — confirming that income/trade evidence is regulated personal information requiring the eight lawful-processing conditions, but is not elevated to special-PI status.
- quote: "\"personal information\" means information relating to an identifiable, living, natural person ... including, but not limited to— ... (b) information relating to the education or the medical, financial, criminal or employment history of the person; ... \"special personal information\" means personal information as referred to in section 26"
- url: https://www.saflii.org/za/legis/consol_act/popia4o2013399/
- source_type: primary
- published: consolidated to 1 February 2022; assented 19 November 2013; G. 37067 of 26 November 2013
- confidence: high

### [12] POPIA s18 requires the responsible party, before (or when) collecting personal information directly from the data subject, to take reasonably practicable steps to ensure awareness of what is collected, the responsible party's name and address, the purpose of collection, whether supply is voluntary or mandatory, consequences of failure, any law authorising collection, recipients, rights of access/rectify/objection, and the right to complain to the Information Regulator — a WhatsApp POI intake must carry this notice up front to survive POPIA scrutiny.
- quote: "If personal information is collected, the responsible party must take reasonably practicable steps to ensure that the data subject is aware of— (a) the information being collected ...; (b) the name and address of the responsible party; (c) the purpose for which the information is being collected; (d) whether or not the supply of the information by that data subject is voluntary or mandatory; (e) the consequences of failure to provide the information; ... (v) right to lodge a complaint to the Information Regulator and the contact details of the Information Regulator"
- url: https://www.saflii.org/za/legis/consol_act/popia4o2013399/
- source_type: primary
- published: s18 commencement 1 July 2020
- confidence: high

### [13] POPIA lawful-processing core for income evidence is Condition 2–3: processing only with consent or another s11 ground (contract performance, legitimate interests), minimality under s10, collection for a specific explicit purpose under s13, and retention no longer than necessary under s14 — a 72-hour demo should collect only the evidence needed for the stated POI purpose and state a short retention/deletion policy.
- quote: "Personal information may only be processed if, given the purpose for which it is processed, it is adequate, relevant and not excessive. ... Personal information may only be processed if— (a) the data subject ... consents to the processing; (b) processing is necessary to carry out actions for the conclusion or performance of a contract ...; (f) processing is necessary for pursuing the legitimate interests of the responsible party ... Personal information must be collected for a specific, explicitly defined and lawful purpose ... records of personal information must not be retained any longer than is necessary for achieving the purpose for which the information was collected"
- url: https://www.saflii.org/za/legis/consol_act/popia4o2013399/
- source_type: primary
- published: Chapter 3 conditions commencement 1 July 2020
- confidence: high

### [14] POPIA (Act 4 of 2013) is administered by the Information Regulator (South Africa); most operative sections (including s18 notification and Chapter 3 conditions) commenced 1 July 2020, with the one-year grace period ending 30 June 2021 — a GKHACK26 demo must already look POPIA-compliant, with no transitional excuse.
- quote: "Sections 2 to 38; 55 to 109; 111; and 114(1), (2) and (3) shall commence on 01 July 2020 and sections 110 and 114(4) shall commence on 30 June 2021. ... It was 1 July 2020, and the one-year grace period to comply ended on 30 June 2021."
- url: https://inforegulator.org.za/acts/
- source_type: primary
- published: GG 43461 / RG 11136 P 21, 22 Jun 2020 (commencement notice); grace-period statement from popia.co.za
- confidence: high

### [15] The NCR's own guidance catalogue includes both "Proposed guidelines for ascertaining consumer's gross and discretionary incomes" and (June 2021) an "Alternative Data Landscape in South Africa" study — primary regulator recognition that informal/alternative income evidence and gross-income ascertainment are live policy topics for affordability, supporting a POI-evidence (not score) product thesis.
- quote: "Proposed guidelines for ascertaining consumer's gross and discretionary incomes - for public comment ... Alternative Data Landscape in South Africa"
- url: https://www.ncr.org.za/index.php/publications/guidelines
- source_type: primary
- published: guidelines catalogue current as of fetch 2026-09-24; Alternative Data Landscape dated June 2021 on https://www.ncr.org.za/index.php/publications/research-reports
- confidence: medium

### [16] Fastest credible 72-hour WhatsApp POI demo consistent with the above is an evidence-pack collector (not a score): POPIA s18 notice + s11 consent at first message, then vendor uploads till slips / bank-statement photos / stokvel subscription records / signed income affidavit / buyer attestations; output is a time-stamped income-evidence dossier for the vendor or a consenting underwriter (NCA s81), with no multi-consumer bureau database and no creditworthiness rating (avoid s43/s157C).
- quote: "A credit provider must not enter into a credit agreement without first taking reasonable steps to assess— ... (iii) existing financial means, prospects and obligations" (income assessment duty sits with the lender; product supplies evidence, not a rating)
- url: https://www.saflii.org/za/legis/consol_act/nca34o2005171/
- source_type: primary
- published: n/a (product implication derived from NCA ss43, 70, 81 and POPIA ss11, 18, 26)
- confidence: medium
