# F5: Academic and institutional evidence that informal/cash-only vendors in South Africa (or comparable African markets) lack credit histories, and that alternative data, community trust, or platform reputation can substitute for bureau files when underwriting micro/small trade credit.

## Findings

### [1] Hundreds of millions in low-income economies cannot obtain a credit score because they lack sufficient credit history, so alternative data is needed as a substitute for bureau files.
- quote: "Hundreds of millions of people in low-income economies do not have a credit or bank account because they have insufficient credit history for a credit score to be ascribed to them."
- url: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.eswa.2020.113766
- source_type: secondary
- published: 2020-12 (Expert Systems with Applications, 2021 issue; Djeundje, Crook, Calabrese, Hamid, Univ. of Edinburgh Credit Research Centre)
- confidence: high

### [2] Models combining email-usage and psychometric variables with demographics can reach predictive accuracy high enough to underwrite new-to-credit borrowers when conventional credit history is unavailable.
- quote: "a model that contains email usage and psychometric variables, as well as demographic variables, can give greater predictive accuracy than a model that uses demographic data only and that the predictive accuracy is sufficiently high for the demographic and email data to be used when conventional credit history data is unavailable."
- url: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.eswa.2020.113766
- source_type: secondary
- published: 2020-12 (Djeundje et al., Expert Systems with Applications vol. 164, art. 113766)
- confidence: high

### [3] Underbanked borrowers in emerging markets cannot supply traditional collateral or identity documents required by banks, which is a structural reason cash-only informal traders are unscorable.
- quote: "in emerging markets, the underbanked individuals cannot access traditional forms of collateral or identification that is required by financial institutions for them to be granted loans."
- url: https://doi.org/10.3390/ijfs9030039
- source_type: secondary
- published: 2021-07 (Mhlanga, International Journal of Financial Studies 9(3):39; University of Johannesburg)
- confidence: high

### [4] Machine-learning credit models that consume alternative/public data can reduce information asymmetry and verify repayment ability for borrowers who lack formal credit files, enabling lending to financially excluded households.
- quote: "artificial intelligence and machine learning have a strong impact on credit risk assessments using alternative data sources such as public data to deal with the problems of information asymmetry, adverse selection, and moral hazard."
- url: https://doi.org/10.3390/ijfs9030039
- source_type: secondary
- published: 2021-07 (Mhlanga, International Journal of Financial Studies 9(3):39)
- confidence: high

### [5] African SMEs (including informal micro/trade firms) are locked out of formal credit by information asymmetry, lack of collateral, perceived high default risk, and the absence of credit bureaus in developing countries.
- quote: "the absence of credit bureaus in developing countries also contribute to the failure to access finance from formal institutions."
- url: https://doi.org/10.3390/jrfm15060270
- source_type: secondary
- published: 2022-06 (Mpofu & Sibindi, Journal of Risk and Financial Management 15(6):270; University of South Africa)
- confidence: high

### [6] Policy research on African informal SME finance explicitly recommends linking formal and informal finance and having SMEs use FinTech platforms to access credit — i.e. bridges from informal trust networks to underwritable data.
- quote: "the establishment of a suitable regulatory framework which will nurture the informal finance sector while promoting consumer protection, and (2) linking the formal and informal sector. On the other hand, SMEs should improve their risk management practices and also embrace FinTech platforms in order to access credit."
- url: https://doi.org/10.3390/jrfm15060270
- source_type: secondary
- published: 2022-06 (Mpofu & Sibindi, JRFM 15(6):270)
- confidence: high

### [7] South African stokvels (ROSCAs) are a large parallel credit/savings system estimated at R49 billion with 11.5 million members — community-held repayment and contribution history that sits entirely outside credit bureaus.
- quote: "Stokvel is a type of 'rotating savings and credit associations' found only in South Africa (SA) which have an estimated value of R49 billion and a membership of 11,5 million, in a country with a populace of 57million and an informal economy with a total value of R160 billion – yet remain a hidden sector of the economy."
- url: https://doi.org/10.21511/ppm.17(4).2019.03
- source_type: secondary
- published: 2019 (Bophela & Khumalo, Problems and Perspectives in Management 17(4))
- confidence: high

### [8] Cape Town grocery stokvels operate formal constitutions with enforceable penalties (85% of groups), fixed monthly contributions (ZAR200–ZAR1500 across 10–30 members), and are 95% women-led — documented community governance that can serve as a trust/reputation score for micro trade credit.
- quote: "Stokvels comprised 10-30 members contributing ZAR200-ZAR1500 monthly. All operated under formal constitutions, with 85% enforcing penalties for rule breaches."
- url: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jneb.2026.03.013
- source_type: secondary
- published: 2026 (Lukwa, Wayas, Lambert & Alaba, Journal of Nutrition Education and Behavior; DOI 10.1016/j.jneb.2026.03.013)
- confidence: high

### [9] Historical African informal credit systems secured loans with social reputation and reciprocity rather than formal collateral or bureau files — the conceptual precedent for community-trust underwriting now used by stokvels and trader networks.
- quote: "Credit transactions were thus regulated by trust, reciprocity, and customary law, with social reputation functioning as the primary form of security rather than formal collateral."
- url: https://doi.org/10.37745/ijhphr.13/vol14n12836
- source_type: secondary
- published: 2026 (Opara, International Journal of History and Philosophical Research)
- confidence: medium

### [10] Even financially included South African households keep using informal instruments alongside formal ones because informal credit is valued for flexibility, speed, trust, and social embeddedness — evidence that community trust is an active substitute/complement to bureau-scored credit.
- quote: "formal products serving structured and long-term financial goals, while informal instruments remained valuable for flexibility, speed, trust, social embeddedness and crisis response."
- url: https://doi.org/10.1108/ijbm-11-2025-0959
- source_type: secondary
- published: 2026 (Lappeman, Nordoff, Daly & Cheteni, International Journal of Bank Marketing)
- confidence: medium

### [11] FinMark Trust’s FinScope work is designed to measure the informal financial product/service market, and its MSME formalisation programme states that informal traders remain outside formal finance because of registration barriers and weak incentives to formalise.
- quote: "The FinScope Consumer Survey is uniquely aimed at increasing understanding of the informal financial product/service market."
- url: https://www.finmark.org.za/
- source_type: primary
- published: 2026-09 (site content as fetched 2026-09-24; FinScope Consumer South Africa 2025 media release dated 30 Jul 2026)
- confidence: high

### [12] FinMark Trust reports that only 27% of SADC citizens access digital financial services, underlining the thinness of digital transaction trails that alternative-data scoring would currently rely on in southern Africa.
- quote: "evidence indicates that only 27% of SADC citizens access digital financial services."
- url: https://www.finmark.org.za/
- source_type: primary
- published: 2026-09 (FinMark Trust Digitalisation programme page, fetched 2026-09-24)
- confidence: high

### [13] Mobile money is documented as a workable financial rail for SMEs in markets where most firms lack bank access (Douala, Cameroon; 285 surveyed SMEs + 12 interviews), supporting the claim that platform/payment data can stand in for bank-relationship history.
- quote: "the findings validate the role that mobile money plays in promoting the inclusive finance agenda for SMEs, mainly in the context of emerging economies where the majority of people and businesses do not have access to banking services"
- url: https://doi.org/10.3390/joitmc6040163
- source_type: secondary
- published: 2020-12 (Tengeh & Talom, Journal of Open Innovation: Technology, Market, and Complexity 6(4):163; Cape Peninsula University of Technology / Douala SMEs)
- confidence: medium
